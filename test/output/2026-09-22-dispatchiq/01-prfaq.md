# Press Release

**DispatchIQ Ends the Guessing Game for Home-Service Customers and Dispatchers**
Small plumbing, electrical, and HVAC businesses get auto-routed job scheduling and automatic customer text updates, without hiring an office manager just to manage the phone.

[CITY, STATE] -- April 5, 2027 -- DispatchIQ launches today, a scheduling and dispatch tool for home-service businesses with a handful of technicians in the field. It replaces the paper calendar and the all-day phone calls with a schedule that routes jobs to the nearest available technician and texts customers automatically when their technician is on the way.

Dispatchers at small plumbing, electrical, and HVAC shops spend hours a day calling customers to confirm appointments and explain delays. A missed call means a missed appointment, and a missed appointment means an angry customer and a wasted slot. Existing scheduling tools are built for consumer calendars and don't account for drive time between jobs. Enterprise dispatch software costs more than most small shops can justify for a handful of trucks.

DispatchIQ auto-routes each new job to the technician who can get there soonest, accounting for drive time and current job load, and sends the customer a text the moment a technician is on the way, with a live ETA. Dispatchers set up a job in under a minute; the rest happens without another phone call. Technicians see their route on their phone.

"Our dispatcher used to make forty calls a day just confirming appointments," said the founder of a pilot HVAC customer's shop, speaking through DispatchIQ's founder. "Now she calls only when something's wrong."

"I don't have to guess whether my technician remembered the address," said a dispatcher at a pilot customer's HVAC company. "The system already told the customer where he is."

DispatchIQ is available now for home-service businesses with 2 to 15 technicians, starting with a paid pilot this quarter ahead of general availability in Q2 2027.

## External FAQ

**How much does DispatchIQ cost?**
Per-technician-seat subscription pricing, tiered by shop size, plus SMS usage beyond a monthly included allotment. [PRICING_DETAIL] to be finalized before general availability.

**Do I need to change how I schedule jobs today?**
No new hardware. Dispatchers use a web dashboard; technicians use a mobile view. Existing paper or calendar-based workflows migrate in during onboarding.

**What happens if a technician is running late?**
The system automatically updates the customer's ETA text when a technician's location indicates a delay.

**Does this replace my existing invoicing or payment system?**
Not at launch. DispatchIQ handles scheduling, routing, and customer notification only.

**What are the top risks?**
SMS delivery silently failing is the single biggest risk: it's the entire value proposition, and a silent failure looks identical to a working system from the dispatcher's side unless something is actively watching for it. A second risk: the team currently has no way to find out about a production failure except a customer emailing support the next morning, an operational gap today.

**What needs to be true for this to succeed?**
The paid pilot has to produce evidence that auto-routing and automatic notification measurably reduce no-shows and missed appointments. One shop's anecdote isn't enough; the pilot needs a before/after comparison a dispatcher can point to.

**Timeline and investment required.**
Paid pilot this quarter, general availability Q2 2027. Team is the founder plus one contractor engineer today; budget is tight and pre-revenue, with roughly six months of runway, loosening if the pilot converts to paying accounts at the rate the MRR target assumes.
