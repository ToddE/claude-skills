# Press Release

**ClassPods Helps Teachers Run Small-Group Classwork Where Every Student Actually
Participates**

A classroom tool for K-12 teachers that builds balanced groups, rotates roles, and shows
which groups need a check-in, all in real time.

Overlook, OH -- January 12, 2027 -- ClassPods, a tool for K-12 teachers running
small-group classwork, is available today for grades 3-8 classrooms in schools with a 1:1
iPad program.

Teachers who assign small-group work often watch the same one or two students take over
every group, while other students settle into a passive role and stay there for the rest
of the year. Building groups by hand from memory or a spreadsheet, teachers rarely have
time to balance them by skill level or by who did the work last time. Once a session
starts, there's no way to tell which groups are actually working until the teacher walks
over to a table, and for a class of six groups in a 40-minute period, most groups get
checked once, if at all.

ClassPods builds a group roster from a teacher's own class list and past session data,
which the teacher can override in seconds, and assigns roles, facilitator, recorder,
timekeeper, presenter, that rotate automatically so the same student isn't always the one
holding the pen. During the session, the teacher taps to log who's contributing directly
from their own iPad while circulating the room, and a shared view shows which groups
haven't been checked on recently. No audio or video is recorded.

"I taught fifth grade for nine years, and every single year, without meaning to, I let the
same three kids run every group project because it was faster than fixing the groups
myself," said Mia Alvarez, founder and CEO of ClassPods. "This is the tool I wanted as a
teacher: something that rebalances groups and rotates roles for me, so the quiet kid isn't
permanently the one who just watches."

"By week four of the pilot, I could walk into any classroom during group work and actually
tell which groups needed me, instead of guessing based on which table was loudest," said a
pilot teacher at Overlook Elementary.

Individual teachers can start a free trial today at classpods.example.com. Districts
interested in a building-wide or district-wide license can contact ClassPods directly for
pricing.

# FAQ

## External FAQ

**What does it cost?**
Free for an individual teacher on a single-classroom trial. District and building-wide
licenses are priced per teacher seat annually, with a discounted rate at 100+ seats;
contact ClassPods for a quote.

**How does it work day to day?**
A teacher imports or syncs a class roster, sets group size, and ClassPods proposes groups
balanced by the participation history logged in prior sessions. The teacher can override
any grouping before starting. During the session, roles rotate automatically and the
teacher taps a simple log to mark contribution per group.

**What devices does it need?**
An iPad (or other tablet) per teacher for the live view and logging. Students don't need a
device for ClassPods itself, though the classwork they're doing in their groups may use
one separately.

**Does it record audio or video of students?**
No. Participation is logged only by the teacher's own tap, on purpose, to avoid recording
or monitoring students directly.

**Is student data safe?**
Yes. ClassPods stores only roster identifiers and participation counts needed to balance
groups, operates under a data processing agreement with each district, and does not sell
data or use it for advertising. See the Internal FAQ for how this is handled as an
education record under [COMPLIANCE_REQUIREMENT: FERPA and applicable state student data
privacy law].

**When is this available?**
Pilot with three elementary schools begins Q1 2027. General availability for districts is
targeted for Q3 2027, aligned to the start of the school year.

## Internal FAQ

**How big is the market?**
Roughly 3.7 million K-12 teachers in the U.S. A meaningfully addressable core, general
education teachers in grades 3-8 in 1:1 iPad districts who assign small-group classwork
at least weekly, is estimated at [METRIC: ~350,000 teachers], before any international
expansion.

**What's the competitive landscape?**
Seesaw and ClassDojo cover classroom communication and behavior tracking; Google Classroom
covers assignment distribution and grading. None of them handle live, in-session group
formation or participation-weighted regrouping. The closest substitute is a teacher's own
spreadsheet and judgment, which is the actual competitor to displace.

**What are the unit economics?**
Target price is [METRIC: $79/teacher/year] at list, [METRIC: $5/teacher/year] at
100+-seat district volume. Marginal cost per teacher is low: no audio or video storage,
just roster identifiers and small participation-event records. Primary costs are
engineering, support, and district-facing sales, not infrastructure.

**What has to be true for this to succeed?**
Teachers have to keep up the one-tap logging habit during live instruction without it
feeling like extra administrative work, and the balanced-grouping and role-rotation
mechanism has to produce a difference teachers can actually see within a grading period,
not just a difference on paper.

**Top reasons this could fail:**
Teachers abandon the tap-logging habit once the pilot excitement wears off, so the data
behind "balanced" groups goes stale. District procurement operates on an annual budget
cycle, so a slow pilot-to-contract conversion could burn the runway needed to reach
general availability. Balanced groups and visible participation could turn out to change
teachers' perception without changing actual student learning outcomes, which would
undercut the pitch to a district evaluating it on outcomes rather than adoption.

**Compliance and data handling:**
Participation records tied to student roster identities are an education record under
FERPA, handled through a school-official exception and a data processing agreement per
district, not a general-audience product exempt from student privacy obligations.
[COMPLIANCE_REQUIREMENT: several states, e.g. California under SOPIPA-successor law and
New York under Education Law 2-d, impose additional student data privacy requirements
beyond FERPA that will need per-state review before district sales in those states.]

3,000 active teacher accounts across at least 40 districts. At least 70% of teachers who
start using ClassPods are still running two or more sessions per week by week eight of
use. At least 60% of surveyed teachers report a normally-disengaged student was visibly
more engaged during group work by the end of a full grading period.

Pilot: Q1 2027, three elementary schools. General availability: Q3 2027, timed to the
