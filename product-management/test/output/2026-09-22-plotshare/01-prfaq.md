# Press Release

**PlotShare Brings Digital Plot Scheduling and Surplus Sharing to City Community Gardens**
Community garden members get a schedule everyone can check from their phone, and a way to route extra harvest to neighbors and food banks instead of letting it rot.

[CITY, STATE] -- April 6, 2027 -- The Parks Department is launching PlotShare, an app that replaces the paper sign-up sheet and scattered group texts community garden members currently use to coordinate plot access and surplus produce.

Today, plot scheduling in most city gardens runs on a sign-up sheet pinned to a shed, or a group text thread half the members never joined. Members show up to find someone else already at their plot, or miss a slot entirely because they never saw the message. Surplus produce, more than any one household can use, mostly goes to waste: nobody's tracking who has extra, and nobody's coordinating a food bank pickup before it spoils.

PlotShare gives every member a schedule they can check and claim from their phone, visible to the whole garden instead of whoever happened to see a text. A surplus board lets members list extra produce for neighbors to claim, and automatically notifies a food bank contact when a listing goes unclaimed, so routing surplus doesn't depend on someone remembering to make a call. Members without a smartphone still get a printed version of the schedule generated from the app, so the paper sheet isn't going away for anyone who needs it.

"Members have been asking for something better than a shed door covered in sign-up sheets for years," said the Parks Department's community gardens coordinator. "PlotShare gives every garden the same tool without asking any one garden to build it themselves."

"I used to lose half my tomatoes because I didn't know who to give them to before they went bad," said a community garden member from the pilot garden. "Now I list them in the app and they're gone within a day, usually to someone two plots over."

PlotShare launches at the pilot garden this growing season, with a rollout to additional city gardens planned as the pilot proves out. Community garden members can ask their garden coordinator whether their garden is part of the rollout.

# FAQ

## External FAQ

**What does PlotShare cost?**
Nothing to garden members. It's a Parks Department service, not a commercial product.

**Do I need a smartphone to use it?**
No. Anyone without one gets a printed schedule generated from the app, kept at the garden shed the way the current sign-up sheet is.

**What happens to a surplus listing nobody claims?**
After a set window, it's flagged for the garden's food bank contact automatically, so unclaimed surplus doesn't just sit there.

**Can my garden get PlotShare if it's not the pilot garden?**
Not yet. The rollout follows the pilot; ask your garden coordinator about timing for your garden.

## Internal FAQ

**How is success measured here, since this isn't a revenue product?**
Three measures, all tied to the pilot growing season: at least 70% of the pilot garden's members actively using the schedule feature, at least 200 lbs of surplus produce routed to the food bank that would otherwise have gone to waste, and a drop in plot-scheduling disputes reported to the garden coordinator compared to the prior season.

**What's the alternative landscape?**
General scheduling apps and generic community bulletin board apps exist, but none combine plot scheduling with a surplus-to-food-bank pipeline built for how community gardens actually operate, including the no-smartphone fallback.

**What resources does this require?**
Department engineering time to build and maintain the app, printing costs for the paper fallback, and the existing garden coordinator's time to onboard members. No new staff role required for the pilot.

**What are the top risks?**
Low adoption if members don't trust a new system over the shed door they know. Mitigation: keep the printed fallback so nobody's forced onto the app, and have the garden coordinator personally walk members through it at the season kickoff meeting. A second risk: the food bank contact's own capacity to actually collect flagged surplus; mitigation is confirming that capacity before launch, not assuming it.

**What needs to be true for this to succeed?**
The garden coordinator has to actively promote it at the season kickoff, not just make it available. Passive availability without that push is the most likely way this quietly fails to get adopted.

**Timeline and resources required.**
Pilot launches next growing season. Ongoing cost is minimal: hosting, printing, and the coordinator's existing time. No dedicated budget line beyond what the department already spends coordinating gardens.
